## The course

Over seven weeks you turn an interest in speech technology into a research proposal you can take into your master's thesis. You learn to ask a focused research question, search and read the literature systematically, find a gap worth filling, and design a study that's ethical, feasible and repeatable. Then you explain it to specialists and non-specialists alike, in a blog post, a poster and a short talk.

## How it works

Classes are seminars. We sit in a circle, and most classes follow the same rhythm: a short introduction, two 30-minute activities with a break in between, and a wrap-up. Every activity uses your own project, so each class moves your proposal forward.

Research design is a practice, and it's learned by doing it. So you meet each problem first and get the concepts afterwards. You rebuild a classmate's LEGO model from their written instructions and find out what your methods section leaves out. You decide speech technology dilemmas in a card game and see which values you give up. You fund each other's posters with play money and have to say why. The discussion after each game is where the learning happens.

Four frameworks carry you from question to proposal: [FARES]({{ '/frameworks/fares/' | relative_url }}) for research questions, [PRISMA]({{ '/frameworks/prisma/' | relative_url }}) for searching, [MERC]({{ '/frameworks/merc/' | relative_url }}) for reading critically and [TIPS]({{ '/frameworks/tips/' | relative_url }}) for gaps. Classmates review your work throughout, and you review theirs.
