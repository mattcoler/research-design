# Research proposal

**Weight:** 45%

**Due:** {% include deadline.html id="proposal_due" %} (exact date and time in BrightSpace)

**Assigned:** Class 9, {% include classdate.html n="9" %}

**Length:** 3,500-4,500 words, excluding title page, references and appendices

**Builds on:** Checkpoint 2, your learning log and the work from Classes 2-11

## Purpose

Write the proposal for the study you would carry out as your master's thesis. A reader should finish it knowing what you will ask, why it matters, what you expect to find, how you will find out, and why your plan is realistic. You are not reporting results: this is a plan, argued well enough that a supervisor or an ethics committee could approve it.

Most of the material already exists. Class 2 gave you your RQ, and Checkpoint 2 gives you the search, analysis, synthesis and gaps. Class 6 gave you a hypothesis and the chain from gap to result; Class 7 an ethics choice; Class 8 a method, risks and a pilot; Class 9 a revision plan; Class 11 a data plan. The proposal pulls these into one argument and fills in what's missing.

## How to submit

Write the proposal directly in Google Docs. Continuing in your Checkpoint 2 document is fine, and makes the history easier to follow. Share the link in BrightSpace with View access before the deadline, so I can see the full version history: when text was added, revised, moved or deleted. The assignment assesses how you develop and refine the proposal, as well as the final text. Documents that show a single bulk insertion of text or minimal revision will not be accepted for full credit.

- ✅ Good signs: writing over many sessions, visible revision and reorganisation, sections that change after feedback
- ⚠️ Warning signs: large blocks inserted at once, almost no edits, perfectly formatted on first draft
- 🚩 Red flags: whole sections pasted in, no revision marks, polished prose throughout from the start

If you use AI tools, add the AI usage statement described in our [class AI policy]({{ '/syllabus/' | relative_url }}#ai-policy) as an appendix.

## What to submit

Word ranges are guides. Spend words where your project needs them.

### 1. Title and abstract (150-200 words)

A specific title, and an abstract that states the problem, the research question, the hypothesis or expected outcome, the method and the expected contribution.

### 2. Introduction and research question (400-600 words)

- The problem and who it affects, with evidence
- Your research question, revised since Class 2 and checked against FARES (Focused, Answerable, Relevant, Empirical, Significant)
- Sub-questions, if you have them, and what is out of scope

### 3. Literature review and gap (1,000-1,300 words)

- Organised by theme: what the field agrees on, where studies disagree and why, how methods have shifted
- Critical, using MERC: where the evidence is strong, where it is thin, and what the methods can and can't show
- Ends on the gap your study fills, with a short TIPS justification (Theoretical significance, Impact, Practical feasibility, Strategic positioning)

Revise your Checkpoint 2 text; don't paste it in unchanged.

### 4. Hypothesis or expected outcome (200-300 words)

- A testable hypothesis that follows from the gap, with the reason you expect it (Class 6)
- How each concept will be measured
- The result that would show you wrong

Engineering or exploratory projects may state an expected outcome without a formal hypothesis, but they still need the third point.

### 5. Methods (800-1,100 words)

- **Design:** what kind of study this is, and why it fits the claim you want to make
- **Data:** what you will use or collect, where it comes from, and who decides you may use it
- **Participants or materials:** who or what, how many, how recruited or selected
- **Procedure and analysis:** detailed enough that someone else could repeat it
- **Pilot:** the assumption you'll test first, how, and what result would change your plan (Class 8)
- **Alternatives:** at least one method you considered and rejected, and why

### 6. Ethics and data management (250-400 words)

- The main ethical risk in your study and the design choice that addresses it, in the form from Class 7: "Because \[harm\], I will \[design choice\], which also \[improves the data, sampling or evaluation\]"
- Which Campus Fryslân Ethics Committee route applies, and why
- Personal data: what you collect (voice recordings usually count), consent, storage, access and deletion

### 7. Feasibility, risks and timeline (250-400 words)

- Your two or three biggest risks, how you'd notice each one early, and your Plan B
- A month-by-month timeline for the thesis, with the pilot, ethics clearance and writing time shown (a table is fine)
- Resources you need: data access, compute, equipment, participants, supervision

### 8. Expected contribution (150-250 words)

What the field will know, or be able to do, after your study, whichever way the result comes out.

### 9. References (not in word count)

APA 7th edition, one alphabetised list. Every in-text citation appears here; every entry is cited in the text.

### Appendices (not in word count)

- **Revision note (150-250 words):** the most important changes since Checkpoint 2, and which peer or instructor feedback prompted them. Name one comment you decided not to act on, and why.
- AI usage statement, if applicable
- Optional: pilot materials, consent form draft, data management table

## Rubric (20 points)

Each criterion is scored at three levels: full points when it is fully met, half when it is partly met or thin, zero when it is missing.

- **Question and framing (3):** a focused, answerable question that passes FARES; the problem is motivated with evidence; scope is clear.
- **Literature and gap (4):** thematic and critical; agreements, disagreements and method limits are explained; the gap follows from the review and is justified with TIPS.
- **Hypothesis and alignment (3):** a testable hypothesis or expected outcome with a stated reason; concepts are operationalised; gap, question, hypothesis, method and falsifying result connect.
- **Methods (4):** the design fits the claim; data, sample, procedure and analysis are specific and repeatable; a sensible pilot; alternatives considered.
- **Ethics and data (2):** specific risks with design responses; correct ethics route; personal data handled properly.
- **Feasibility (2):** realistic timeline with pilot and ethics clearance in it; real risks with early signs and Plan B.
- **Writing and references (1):** clear structure and prose; APA references correct and complete; within the word range (±10%).
- **Revision (1):** version history shows the proposal developing; the revision note shows considered use of feedback.

## Before you submit

- Read your abstract, then the methods. Do they describe the same study?
- Can a reader find your research question and hypothesis in under a minute?
- Does every claim about the literature have a citation?
- Is the link set to View access, and is it the right document?
