# Learning log

**Weight:** 10% (five entries, 2 points each)

**Format:** handwritten, in the notebook you get in Class 1

**Entries:** Class 1 ({% include classdate.html n="1" fmt="short" %}), Class 7 ({% include classdate.html n="7" fmt="short" %}), DuST ({% include tripdate.html fmt="short" %}), Class 11 ({% include classdate.html n="11" fmt="short" %}), Class 13 ({% include classdate.html n="13" fmt="short" %})

**Submit:** a photo of each entry in BrightSpace before you leave the room; the DuST entry by {% include deadline.html id="log_dust" fmt="medium" time="y" %}

## Purpose

Each game in this course gives you an experience. The log is where that experience becomes something you can use: you write down what happened, work out what it showed you, and decide what changes in your own project. Kolb (1984) describes learning from experience as a cycle of four steps: an experience, reflection on it, a new idea, and trying the idea out. Games are good at the first step. The log covers the other three.

Every entry ends with a specific change to your RQ or proposal, so the log feeds straight into the work that's graded.

## Why by hand

Writing by hand is slower, so you have to choose what's worth writing down. It's also the one piece of writing in this course where AI isn't in the room. What's on the page is your own thinking at that moment.

You may have read that handwriting beats typing for memory. The best-known study behind that claim (Mueller & Oppenheimer, 2014) wasn't confirmed by a larger direct replication (Urry et al., 2021), so it isn't our reason. What does have support is the kind of prompt: writing helps learning most when the prompts ask you to think about your own thinking (Bangert-Drowns et al., 2004). The prompts below are built that way.

## How it works

- The last minutes of Classes 1, 7, 11 and 13 are set aside for the log. At DuST you take notes on the day and finish the entry at home.
- Half a page to a page per entry is plenty. The DuST entry can run to two pages.
- Write in English. Handwriting, spelling and grammar aren't graded.
- Before you leave the room, photograph the entry and upload it to BrightSpace. Keep the notebook: you reread it in Class 13, and it helps w/ the revision note in your proposal.
- If you miss a class with a log entry, do the make-up for that game (see below) and upload the entry within a week.
- I'm the only one who reads your entries.

## Prompts

Every entry answers the same four questions. Each entry adds one or two of its own.

1. What happened? Two or three sentences, facts only.
2. What surprised you, or where did you see it differently from others?
3. What does that mean for your project?
4. What will you change? Name the proposal section or the decision.

## Missed a class?

Do a short version of that game at home, then write the entry by hand in your notebook and upload it within a week of the class. The materials are in BrightSpace.

- Class 1: sort the six scenario cards (A-F) on your own into appropriate, depends and inappropriate, then compare your sort w/ the photo of the class board. Write about where you disagreed with the room.
- Class 7: take three dilemma cards. For each, choose A or B and the value you'd play, then compare w/ the groups' record sheets. Write about a dilemma where your value would have lost.
- Class 11: document a small build (bricks, a paper fold, or a ten-step drawing) in words and sketches only. Give the instructions to someone outside the course and watch them rebuild it without asking questions. Write about where the rebuild first went wrong.
- Class 13: read three classmates' poster PDFs and invest five €20,000 notes, one reason per note. Write about the reason you found hardest to write. Upload by {% include deadline.html id="participation" fmt="medium" %}.
- DuST: tell me beforehand and I'll set an alternative task.

Make-ups are graded the same way as entries written in class.

## Grading

The log is graded for completion. Each entry is worth 2 points:

- **2 points:** answers the prompts and names a specific change. A specific change says where and what, e.g. "add speaker age to the sampling plan in section 5".
- **1 point:** written, but thin, or the change is vague ("improve my methods").
- **0 points:** missing.

Your total out of 10 is your learning log grade, worth 10% of the course grade.
