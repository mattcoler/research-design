# Checkpoint 1

**Weight:** 5% (plus 5% for your peer review of others' checkpoints)

**Assigned:** Class 2, {% include classdate.html n="2" %}

**Due:** {% include deadline.html id="cp1_due" time="y" %}

**Peer review due:** {% include deadline.html id="cp1_review" time="y" %} (FeedbackFruits)

Get your research direction articulated early and start systematic literature discovery. This is a formative checkpoint: the goal is exploration and early feedback, not perfection.

## How to submit

You must write your checkpoint directly in Google Docs and share the link in BrightSpace with View access before the deadline. This is required so I can view the full version history, including when text was added, revised, moved, or deleted over time. This requirement exists because the assignment assesses your ability to develop and refine your research thinking, not only the final product. Documents that show a single bulk insertion of text or minimal revision will not be accepted for full credit.

- ✅ Good signs: incremental writing over multiple sessions, visible deletion/revision, reorganization, gradual refinement
- ⚠️ Warning signs: single large text insertions at the level of sentences, minimal edits, perfectly formatted on first draft, no visible struggle
- 🚩 Red flags: entire sections pasted at once, no revision marks, suspiciously polished prose throughout

If you use AI tools, include a short AI usage statement as described in our [class AI policy]({{ '/syllabus/' | relative_url }}#ai-policy).

## What to submit

### 1. Research positioning (400-600 words)

Tell me:

- What 2-3 areas of speech technology intrigue you? Why do they matter?
- How do your interests connect to your background or experiences?
- Where do you see potential opportunities to contribute?

What I'm looking for:

- Real engagement with the field (cite 2-3 recent papers/developments)
- Honest reflection on your interests (uncertainty is fine!)
- Connection between your curiosities and feasible thesis work

Remember: your direction will evolve. This is a starting point, not a commitment.

### 2. Search strategy (400-600 words + documentation)

Document your literature search approach. Log every search in your own copy of the [search log template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/10lVOSnlg8q8xI0CnXCMTcTmXghzxeEKMmJsQ9ph3E7k/copy) and use its PRISMA counts tab for the numbers in e) below:

**a) Search question:** What preliminary question guides your search? Use the research question you revised in Class 2.

Example: "How do acoustic features of non-native speech affect ASR performance?"

**b) Databases:** Which databases will you search? Why these?

Consider: IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, Scopus, Web of Science, arXiv, others relevant to your topic. Google Scholar is useful for finding papers, but it can't be one of your documented searches (see Class 3).

**c) Search terms:** What search strings are you using?

- Core concepts you're capturing
- Key synonyms and variants
- Example Boolean structure (you can refine this iteratively!)

**d) Inclusion/exclusion criteria:** What will you include/exclude?

- Publication dates, types, languages
- Research methods or populations
- Brief rationale for your choices

**e) Preliminary results:**

- How many total results across databases?
- After removing duplicates?
- Estimated number that seem relevant after skimming titles/abstracts?

Include a PRISMA-style flow diagram (even hand-drawn is fine!) showing your search process and numbers. Template: [prisma-statement.org](http://prisma-statement.org/). We cover search strategy and PRISMA in Class 3 ({% include classdate.html n="3" fmt="medium" %}).

### 3. Preliminary bibliography

List ~10 papers you've identified as most relevant:

- APA format (7th edition)
- One sentence per paper: why is this relevant to your research?

## Assessment

This checkpoint is graded for completion and baseline quality, not perfection. It is also peer-reviewed.

- ✅ Full credit (5%): all components present; real engagement evident; search is systematic and documented; shows serious effort.
- ⚠️ Half credit (2.5%): missing components or minimal effort; search appears haphazard.
- ❌ No credit (0%): not submitted.

You review two classmates' checkpoints in FeedbackFruits. The peer review is a separate 5%, credited when you complete both reviews by {% include deadline.html id="cp1_review" fmt="medium" %}.

You'll receive feedback to guide your Checkpoint 2 work, regardless of grade.
