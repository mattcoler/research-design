# Games

Three games run through the course. You play first; the discussion afterwards is where the learning happens.

## Ethics card game

In Class 7 ({% include classdate.html n="7" fmt="short" %}) you play in groups of three or four. Each dilemma card describes a situation from speech technology research with two options, A and B. Every option costs something, and the game makes you say what.

### How to play (one rules sheet per group)

1. Each player holds the eight value cards. Shuffle the dilemma cards and put them face down.
2. The reader turns over a dilemma and reads it aloud, with both options.
3. Everyone decides A or B, and puts face down the one value card that decided it for them.
4. Reveal together. Anyone who chose differently, or played a different value, explains first (3 minutes).
5. Agree a decision, or record the split. Write down the value that lost.
6. Pass the reader role to the left. About five minutes per dilemma.

There is no right answer on any card. What matters is which value you were willing to give up, and why.

### The eight value cards

| Value | What it stands for |
| :---- | :---- |
| Privacy | Keep people from being identified or exposed |
| Fairness | Every group is served equally well |
| Consent | People agree, knowing what they agree to |
| Scientific quality | The result is valid and generalises |
| Feasibility | It can be done in the time and budget |
| Openness | Others can check, reuse and build on it |
| Benefit | The people affected gain from it |
| Your career | Publishing, funding, finishing on time |

The dilemma cards are handed out in class. In the second half of the class you write a dilemma card from your own project and play it with someone from another group.

## LEGO reproducibility challenge

In Class 11 ({% include classdate.html n="11" fmt="short" %}) you work in pairs, each pair with an identical LEGO kit. The game shows how much a description leaves out, even when its author thinks it's complete.

### Build, document, swap, rebuild

1. **Build and document (10 minutes).** Build something from 15-25 pieces of your kit. Write instructions so another pair could rebuild it. Words and sketches only, no photos. Hide your original under the table while the other pair rebuilds.
2. **Rebuild (10 minutes).** Pass your instructions (not the model) to the next pair; the last pair passes to the first. Rebuild theirs. No questions allowed; note every guess you make.
3. **Compare (10 minutes).** Put original and copy side by side. Where did they differ, and which missing detail caused it?

### From bricks to your methods section

- What was the LEGO equivalent of an unreported preprocessing step?
- Which details felt too obvious to write down, and turned out to matter?
- Where would a shared vocabulary ("2×4 brick", "stud") have saved you?
- Which part of your own study would be hardest to describe this precisely?

The challenge is adapted from the LEGO® Metadata for Reproducibility game pack by Mary Donaldson and Matt Mahon (University of Glasgow, 2019, <https://doi.org/10.36399/gla.pubs.196477>), licensed CC BY 4.0. LEGO® is a trademark of the LEGO Group, which does not sponsor or endorse this course.

## Research Futures

Research Futures runs during the poster session in Class 13 ({% include classdate.html n="13" fmt="short" %}). Every student and guest gets five Research Futures grant notes and invests them in the projects they would fund.

### Rules

- You have €100,000: five notes of €20,000.
- Invest in posters other than your own. Several notes on one poster is fine.
- Write one reason on every note, then put it in the envelope on the poster's board.
- Notes without a reason don't count.

Each note has two lines to fill in: "Invested in (poster by):" and "Because:".
