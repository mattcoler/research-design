# Communication portfolio

**Weight:** 20% (plus 5% for peer review)

**Assigned:** Class 8, Friday 26 February 2027

**Parts and deadlines:**

- Blog post: Sunday 7 March 2027, 23:59
- Peer review of two blog posts: Sunday 14 March 2027, 23:59 (FeedbackFruits)
- Poster PDF: Monday 15 March 2027, 23:59
- Poster clinic: Class 12, Tuesday 16 March (bring a printout or the PDF on a laptop)
- Printed poster and presentation: Class 13, Friday 19 March 2027, in class

## Purpose

Explain the study you are proposing to two audiences: readers outside your field (the blog post) and a mixed room of researchers and guests (the poster and presentation). Both describe a planned study, not completed research.

## 1. Blog post

Write a blog post that explains your research project to a general academic audience: undergraduates in other fields should follow it, and speech technology professionals should find it accurate. Strong posts may be shared on the Campus Fryslân blog, with your permission.

### Writing process

Write the post directly in Google Docs and share the link in BrightSpace with View access, so I can see the full version history. The assignment assesses how you develop an explanation of your research, not only the final text. Documents that show a single bulk insertion of text, or a one-session, start-to-finish writing process, will not be accepted for full credit.

### Requirements

- 350-500 words
- Explains the problem, your question and your planned approach in plain language
- Connects the research to everyday experience or real-world consequences
- Scientifically accurate; any technical term you need is explained in a sentence
- Your own voice, with a clear line through the piece
- At least one copyright-free image or diagram that helps the reader understand, with a caption and credit

## 2. Poster

Design a research poster for your proposal using the #betterposter layout: one main message in large type in the centre, supporting detail at the sides.

### Content

- Your research question and hypothesis, in accessible language
- The key findings from the literature and the gap you address
- An overview of the planned method, including the pilot
- Expected contribution: what changes if the study works, and for whom
- Consistent with your current proposal draft

### Format

- A0 portrait (841 × 1189 mm), PDF
- Readable from two metres: main message large enough to read across a room
- A balance of text and visuals; every figure earns its place
- You may depart from the #betterposter layout if you can justify it and it works

Upload the PDF to BrightSpace by Monday 15 March. Bring the printed poster to Class 13.

## 3. Poster presentation

- A 5-minute talk at your poster, for a mixed audience
- Be ready for questions from staff, guests and classmates
- During the session, evaluate the three classmates' posters assigned to you, on the form provided. Your scores count towards their presentation mark

## Peer review (5%)

- Blog post: review two classmates' posts in FeedbackFruits by Sunday 14 March
- Poster: feedback slips in Class 12 and the evaluation form in Class 13

Write the comments you'd want to receive: specific (point to a sentence or element), explained (say why), and doable (suggest a fix or a question to answer).

## Rubric (20 points)

Each criterion is scored at three levels: full points when it is fully met, half when it is partly met or thin, zero when it is missing.

- **Blog post (8):** accurate and accessible explanation of the problem, question and approach (3); clear structure and own voice (2); link to everyday relevance (1); image that aids understanding, with credit (1); within 350-500 words and developed over time in the version history (1).
- **Poster (8):** one clear main message (2); question, gap and method correct and consistent with the proposal draft (3); visual design that supports reading at a distance (2); file submitted on time in the required format (1).
- **Presentation (4):** clear 5-minute explanation for a mixed audience (2); answers questions accurately and openly (2). The mark is the average of my score and the mean of your classmates' scores on the evaluation forms.

Use of AI tools follows the [class AI policy](https://mattcoler.github.io/research-design/ai-policy/); include a short AI usage statement at the end of the blog post document if you used them.
